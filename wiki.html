01

Advertising Fundamentals

The words that show up in almost every conversation, every report, every deck. Start here.

Buying Models

CPM (Cost Per Mille) — What you pay per 1,000 impressions, full stop — click or no click, action or no action. It's how most display, video, and CTV inventory gets priced, and it's a measure of reach, not results.

CPC (Cost Per Click) — You pay when someone clicks. Nothing more, nothing less. The standard model in search, where the click is the whole point.

CPA (Cost Per Acquisition) — What each conversion costs you — a sale, a lead, a sign-up — calculated after the fact (spend ÷ conversions). Usually the number a campaign lives or dies by.

CPV (Cost Per View) — What you pay per video view, mainly on YouTube and other video platforms. "View" is the platform's call — often 30 seconds watched, or a click, whichever hits first.

CPCV (Cost Per Completed View) — Same idea as CPV, but you only pay for full watches. Common in CTV and premium video buys.

vCPM (Viewable CPM) — A CPM that only counts impressions that actually had a chance to be seen, not every impression served.

Flat-Rate / Fixed Placement — One price, one placement (a homepage takeover, say), no matter how many impressions or clicks it drives.

PMP (Private Marketplace) — An invite-only auction. A publisher opens up inventory to a select group of advertisers at a negotiated rate, instead of the open market.

Programmatic Guaranteed — Price and volume locked in ahead of time, like a traditional direct buy — just delivered through a programmatic pipe instead of an insertion order.

Core Metrics

Impressions — How many times an ad was served. Not seen, not noticed — served. That distinction matters.

Reach — How many unique people, devices, or households saw the ad at least once.

Frequency — On average, how many times each of those people saw it. Reach × Frequency ≈ total impressions.

CTR (Click-Through Rate) — Clicks ÷ impressions. A rough gauge of how compelling an ad is — though "rough" is doing some work here, since it means different things on search vs. display.

CVR (Conversion Rate) — Conversions ÷ clicks (sometimes impressions). Tells you how well the traffic that shows up actually converts.

Viewability — Whether an ad had the technical shot at being seen. Industry standard (MRC): 50% of pixels in view for 1+ second, 2+ seconds for video.

VCR (Video Completion Rate) — The share of people who watched the whole thing.

Engagement Rate — Likes, comments, shares — divided by impressions or reach. The social-specific measure of "did anyone actually react."

Share of Voice (SOV) — Your slice of total impressions or spend in a category, relative to competitors.

Targeting Concepts

First-Party Data — Data you own — your customers, your site visitors, your CRM.

Third-Party Data — Data someone else collected and you license. Getting harder to come by as privacy rules tighten.

Contextual Targeting — Targeting the page, not the person. Cooking ads on a recipe site — content decides placement, not who's reading.

Behavioral Targeting — Targeting the person, not the page — based on past actions or inferred interests.

Geofencing — Targeting inside a drawn boundary, often a tight radius around a physical location.

Lookalike / Similar Audiences — A new audience built to resemble one you already have — like your past customers, but bigger.

Retargeting / Remarketing — Showing ads to people who already showed you interest — visited the site, added to cart, didn't finish.

Frequency Capping — A ceiling on how many times one person sees the same ad in a given window, so it doesn't wear out its welcome.

Funnel & Strategy Language

Upper / Mid / Lower Funnel — Where a tactic sits in the journey: upper builds awareness, mid builds consideration, lower drives the conversion.

Awareness / Consideration / Conversion — The three stages most media plans are built around, in order.

Brand Lift — The measurable bump in awareness, favorability, or intent a campaign actually caused — usually tracked via survey.

Incrementality — The results that only happened because of the campaign — not the ones that would've happened anyway.

Attribution — How credit for a conversion gets assigned. Last-click hands it all to the final touchpoint; multi-touch spreads it across the path.

A/B Testing — Two versions, one variable, head to head — creative, audience, landing page — to see which one wins.

Creative Rotation / Fatigue — Cycling creative so the same audience doesn't see the same ad so often it stops working.

Programmatic & DSPs

The machinery behind most digital buying. Useful to understand the shape of it, even if the buttons aren't yours to push.

DSP (Demand-Side Platform) — The software agencies use to buy inventory across many publishers at once, usually through automated, real-time bidding.

SSP (Supply-Side Platform) — The publisher's side of the same coin — lets publishers put inventory up for programmatic sale.

Ad Exchange — The marketplace where DSPs and SSPs actually transact. The auction house.

RTB (Real-Time Bidding) — The auction that happens in the milliseconds it takes a page to load.

Header Bidding — Lets publishers shop an impression to multiple exchanges at once before their ad server picks a winner — generally drives the price up in the publisher's favor.

Deal ID — The identifier that ties a specific buyer to a specific negotiated inventory package in a PMP deal.

Open Exchange vs. PMP vs. Programmatic Guaranteed — Three tiers, rising in control and price: open exchange (anyone can bid), PMP (invite-only), programmatic guaranteed (locked in advance).

Brand Safety vs. Brand Suitability — Safety keeps ads away from clearly harmful content. Suitability goes further — matching content to what actually fits a brand, which takes judgment, not a rulebook. If a client's suitability needs aren't obvious, that's a Punch-contact conversation, not a guess.

Viewability Standards (MRC) — The Media Rating Council's industry benchmark for what counts as a viewable impression.

Ad Fraud / IVT (Invalid Traffic) — Bots, click farms, non-human traffic — anything inflating numbers without a real person behind them.

Pixel / Tag — Code on a site that tracks visitor behavior — for measurement, retargeting, or conversions.

Floor Price — The lowest a publisher will take for an impression, full stop.

How-To

What is a DSP, and how does buying through one actually work? — An agency builds a campaign in a DSP — targeting, budget, creative — and the DSP bids on eligible impressions across publishers as pages load, in real time. Which DSP, and how to target it: that's strategy. This is just the mechanism.
DSP landscape overview — A quick, plain-language look at a few major players (The Trade Desk, DV360, Amazon DSP) and broadly what each tends to get used for. Picking one for your account is a Punch-contact conversation.

03

CTV (Connected TV)

TV, but with the targeting and reporting of digital — mostly.

CTV vs. OTT vs. Linear TV — Linear is scheduled broadcast/cable. OTT is anything streamed over the internet. CTV is OTT watched on an actual TV screen — smart TV, Roku, Apple TV, console — not a phone or laptop.

Addressable TV — Different households, same broadcast, different ads — targeted using household-level data. TV's version of digital targeting.

ACR (Automatic Content Recognition) Data — Data smart TVs collect on what's playing, used to measure and target CTV.

Household-Level vs. Device-Level Targeting — CTV usually targets the household (one TV, many viewers), not the device — which changes both how precisely you can target and how you measure it.

VOD (Video on Demand) — Watch it whenever, not on a schedule.

Live vs. On-Demand Inventory — Live programming (sports, events) and on-demand content are priced and sold differently in CTV.

Publisher-Direct vs. Programmatic CTV — Buy directly from a streaming publisher, or buy programmatically alongside everything else. Both are options.

How-To

Why is CTV buying different from traditional TV buying? — CTV brings TV's reach and creative together with digital's targeting and reporting — but it also inherits some real TV headaches, like managing frequency across apps and devices, and measurement that's still catching up to what digital's used to.

YouTube / Online Video

TrueView — YouTube's original skippable in-stream format. You pay only if someone watches long enough or interacts.

Skippable vs. Non-Skippable Ads — Skippable: skip after 5 seconds, advertiser pays only if they don't. Non-skippable: full watch required, typically sold on CPM.

Bumper Ads — Six seconds, no skip button, built for reach — not for saying much.

Content Targeting vs. Audience Targeting — Content targeting picks the video; audience targeting picks the viewer, regardless of what they're watching.

How-To

Overview of YouTube ad formats — A quick rundown of the main formats — skippable in-stream, non-skippable in-stream, bumper, in-feed. Which one fits your campaign is a Punch-contact conversation.
YouTube brand safety controls, explained — What YouTube's built-in safety settings (content exclusions, inventory types) actually do, at a glance.

05

Social

Meta, TikTok, LinkedIn, Pinterest, Snap, X — different platforms, similar bones. Once you know one account structure, the rest are variations.

Account Structure — Meta runs Campaign → Ad Set → Ad. Google and TikTok generally run Campaign → Ad Group → Ad. Budget and targeting usually sit in the middle layer, creative at the bottom.

Pixel / Conversions API — The Pixel tracks visitor actions from the browser side; the Conversions API sends the same kind of data server-side — increasingly the more reliable route as browser tracking gets less dependable.

Custom Audience vs. Lookalike Audience — A custom audience is built from your own data — customer list, site visitors. A lookalike is a new audience the platform builds to resemble it.

Boosted Post vs. Paid Ad — Boosting promotes an existing post with a few simplified settings. A proper paid ad is built with full targeting, placement, and bidding control. Easier vs. precise.

Algorithm-Driven Optimization (e.g., Advantage+, Smart+) — Hand more of the setup — targeting, placement, sometimes creative combinations — to the algorithm. Less manual control, often more efficiency.

Basic Creative Specs — Aspect ratio, file size, video length, text limits — every platform has its own rules, and they shift. Confirm current specs before you finalize creative; don't rely on memory.

How-To

How to get verified on Meta — Verification (Business Manager or Page) runs through Meta Business Suite: legal business name, address, phone number, and supporting documentation — a business license or utility bill — then Meta reviews it. If verification stalls or the requirements look off, that's a Punch-contact conversation, not a guessing game.
How to set up a Meta Business Manager — Create the account at business.facebook.com, add your Page(s) and ad account(s), assign team access. Everything else — verification, Pixel, ad accounts — sits on top of this.
How to install the Meta Pixel / Conversions API — Conceptually: the Pixel is code in a site's header (often via a tag manager) that fires on key events; the Conversions API is its server-side counterpart, set up through Business Manager's Events Manager. The actual implementation is technical and site-specific — bring in your dev team and your Punch contact rather than DIY-ing it from a wiki page.
How to set up a TikTok Business Center — Same idea as Meta Business Manager — one hub for ad accounts, assets, and team permissions, set up through TikTok's Business Center portal.
How to set up LinkedIn Campaign Manager access — Requires a LinkedIn Page, with account access assigned to team members inside Campaign Manager.

Political / issue ad advertiser authorization — This one has requirements that vary by situation — check in with your Punch contact before you move forward.

06

Search

Account Structure (Campaign → Ad Group → Keyword) — Campaigns hold the budget and goal, ad groups hold the theme, keywords and ads sit inside each group.

Quality Score — Google's 1–10 read on ad relevance, expected CTR, and landing page experience. It moves your rank and your cost.

Match Types — Broad match casts the widest net, loosely. Phrase match needs the core meaning intact. Exact match wants a close variant of the exact term. Wider net, less precision — every time.

Search Network vs. Display Network — Search shows up when someone searches for it. Display shows up on partner sites/apps based on targeting, not a query.

Performance Max (PMax) — One campaign, all of Google's inventory — Search, Display, YouTube, Gmail, Maps — run by automation, with less manual control than traditional campaign types.

Smart Bidding — Google's machine-learning bid strategies (Target CPA, Target ROAS), setting bids in real time based on conversion likelihood.

SERP (Search Engine Results Page) — The page you land on after a search — organic results and ads, side by side.

Ad Rank — The formula that decides an ad's position, or whether it shows at all — bid, Quality Score, and expected format impact combined.

Negative Keywords — Terms you explicitly block from triggering your ads, to keep irrelevant traffic out.

How-To

Google Ads account structure, explained — Walks through campaign → ad group → keyword, and why tighter keyword grouping tends to help Quality Score and performance.
Conversion tracking, conceptually — What it is, at a glance: tagging a "success" action so the platform can measure and optimize toward it. Setup is technical and site-specific — bring in Punch and a dev resource for the real implementation.
Google Ads vs. Microsoft/Bing Ads — key differences — Similar mechanics, different audience — Bing skews older with a different device mix, generally lower competition and CPCs, and a few feature gaps versus Google.

Measurement & Report Literacy

The section that answers "what does this number in my report actually mean." Probably the one people use most.

Spend vs. Delivery/Pacing — Spend is money out the door so far. Pacing is whether that spend is on track, ahead, or behind the plan.

WoW / MoM / YoY — Week-over-week, month-over-month, year-over-year. Shorthand for comparing a metric to its prior period.

Benchmark vs. Index — A benchmark is the reference point (industry average CTR, say). An index shows performance relative to it — 120 index means 20% above benchmark.

Data Lag & Attribution Windows — Numbers aren't final the moment they post — conversions can get credited for days after a click or view. That's why very recent data can still move.

Viewability Rate (in a report) — The share of served impressions that met the viewability bar. A quality signal, not a performance metric on its own.

Brand Lift Study — A survey-based study (often run through the platform itself) measuring whether a campaign actually moved awareness or favorability.

Incrementality Test — Isolates what the campaign actually caused, versus what would've happened anyway — usually via a holdout group.

"Optimization" (as a report note) — A deliberate adjustment — targeting, budget, bids, creative — made in response to performance data. Normal, ongoing, not a red flag.

Privacy & Data Basics

Concepts only — jurisdiction-specific rules are a Punch-contact-or-legal conversation, not a wiki answer.

Cookies & the Cookieless Shift — Small browser files, historically used (among other things) for cross-site ad tracking. Third-party cookies have been steadily phased out, pushing the industry toward first-party data and other methods.

IDFA / Apple ATT (App Tracking Transparency) — Apple's rule requiring explicit permission before an app tracks activity across other apps or sites. IDFA is the device ID that permission unlocks — or blocks.

First-Party Data Strategy (general concept) — The industry-wide shift toward brands using their own data — site behavior, CRM, loyalty programs — as third-party data dries up.

Consent Management Platform (CMP) — The tool behind a cookie banner, managing and recording user consent. What it does, conceptually — not what any specific law requires. Anything past this — what a CMP must legally include, how a specific law applies — is a Punch-contact-or-legal conversation.
