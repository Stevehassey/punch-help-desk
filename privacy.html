08 — Privacy Basics

Privacy & Data Basics

The big-picture ideas only. Anything specific to a law or a state is a conversation for your Punch contact or legal team, not this page.

Privacy Law vs. Political Disclosure Law — These are two separate things that are easy to conflate. Privacy law (cookies, tracking consent) governs how user data can be collected and used. Political disclosure law ("paid for by" requirements, public ad-spend files) governs transparency around who's paying for political speech. Both vary by state, and both are changing quickly right now — treat anything specific here as a legal-team question, not a wiki answer.

Cookies & the Cookieless Shift — Cookies are small files a browser stores that, among other things, have historically let advertisers track someone's activity across different websites. Browsers and platforms have been steadily phasing these out, pushing the industry toward first-party data instead.

IDFA / Apple ATT (App Tracking Transparency) — Apple's rule requiring an app to explicitly ask your permission before it's allowed to track your activity across other apps and websites. IDFA is the specific device identifier that permission unlocks — or blocks.

First-Party Data Strategy (general concept) — The industry-wide shift toward relying on data you actually own — a voter file, a donor list, website behavior — as outside data becomes harder to get. Worth noting again: even your own first-party data is subject to the platform-specific targeting restrictions covered on the Social and Search pages.

Consent Management Platform (CMP) — The tool behind a cookie banner — it manages and keeps a record of what a visitor has agreed to. This explains what it does in general terms, not what any specific law requires it to include. Anything past this is a Punch-contact-or-legal conversation.
