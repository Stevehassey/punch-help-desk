Working With Punch

Process FAQ

The practical questions that come up before and during a campaign. For anything account-specific, your Punch contact will have the exact details.

Which platforms even allow political ads?

Currently: Meta, Google (Search/Display/YouTube), X, and Snapchat. TikTok, LinkedIn, Pinterest, and Microsoft/Bing all prohibit political advertising outright, so they're not part of the plan. Platform policies do shift, so this is worth reconfirming each cycle rather than assuming it's unchanged.

What's a "Paid for by" disclaimer, and do we need one?

It's a required statement on the ad itself naming who's responsible for it — standard across every platform that allows political ads. The exact wording rules combine platform policy with federal and state law, so the specific text should be confirmed with your Punch contact and legal team, not copied from a template.

How long does it take to launch a campaign?

Longer than a standard commercial campaign, mainly because of verification: political/election ad authorization on each platform (and often each DSP) can take anywhere from a few days to a few weeks, and it has to happen before anything else. Build that lead time into your timeline from the start. Your Punch contact can give you a specific estimate once they know your plan.

What do you need from us to get started?

Generally: access to (or help creating) your ad accounts, the information needed for political advertiser verification, approved creative with disclaimer text ready, and access to add tracking to your website. The Launch Checklist covers the full list.

How often will we get reports, and what's in them?

Cadence is usually weekly or monthly, depending on the campaign — confirm with your Punch contact. Reports typically cover spend and pacing, key performance metrics, and any notable changes worth flagging.

Who's our point of contact once we're live?

Your dedicated Punch contact stays your main point of contact throughout — for questions, changes, and reporting.

Can we make changes after a campaign launches?

Yes — campaigns are actively managed and adjusted based on performance. Bigger changes (budget, timeline, audience, creative) should go through your Punch contact so they can be planned properly rather than made on the fly.

Is there a blackout period before an election?

On some platforms, yes — Meta, for instance, stops accepting brand-new political ads in the final week before an election, though ads already running can typically keep delivering. These windows differ by platform and aren't always announced far ahead, so confirm current policy close to your date rather than assuming last cycle's rules still apply.

What if we need to pause or end a campaign early?

Usually possible, though notice periods and any minimum commitments depend on your specific agreement. Check with your Punch contact or your contract for the exact terms.

Do we need a long-term contract?

Political engagements are often naturally tied to an election cycle rather than open-ended, so this looks different than a typical commercial retainer. Ask your Punch contact about the options that fit your timeline.

How is budget or billing typically structured?

Agencies commonly work on some combination of media spend plus a management fee, or a flat retainer — the right structure depends on your goals and budget. Your Punch contact can walk through what applies to your account.

Do you handle creative, or do we need our own?

Depends on the engagement — some clients bring their own creative, others want support building it. Worth a direct conversation with your Punch contact about what's included.
